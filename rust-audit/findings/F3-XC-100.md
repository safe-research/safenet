## Claim

Only the constant term of a DKG commitment is bound by a proof of knowledge, so the participant that commits last can choose its other coefficients after seeing every other commitment and make an honest victim's verifying share `Y_v = sum_k F_k(x_v)` the identity. The victim's own validator does not notice: it builds its `keyGenSecretShare` with `y = (0, 0)` and its peers would accept that value, but `FROSTParticipantMap.set` rejects `(0, 0)` with `NotOnCurve`, so the victim's share never lands. If the attacker then withholds its own share, no complaint can be raised against it, and at the share deadline every validator excludes everyone outside `shared`: the attacker and the honest victim. With the Aegis set (5 validators, threshold 3) the remaining 3 are below `min_participants(5) = 4`, so the restart fails and the epoch is skipped. Each new epoch starts again with everyone included, so one malicious validator (within A2's fault bound) can block every rollover for as long as it stays configured, while the evidence onchain points at the victim, whose transaction is the one that reverted. In larger committees where a restart stays viable, the honest victim (up to `threshold - 1` victims) loses its seat instead.

This is a Rust and Solidity mismatch on the identity point (A7): PR #1025 (`7fc1e08`) built the Rust side on the premise that the victim "publishes `y = (0,0)`, which the contract accepts", and `frost/mod.rs` pins that premise in a test, but the contract has rejected `(0, 0)` in `set` since before run 2. Pre-existing at `fe9e84c` (same `set`, same `y` computation, same share-timeout exclusion through `public_keys.keys()`).

## Trigger

Aegis rollover, 5 configured validators, threshold 3, one of them malicious (A2 allows fewer than one third).

1. At the epoch boundary every validator starts the rollover keygen and commits within a few blocks. The attacker waits (the commitment deadline is `key_gen_timeout` = 120 blocks) until the other four `KeyGenCommitted` events are onchain.
2. The attacker runs a normal setup, keeps its valid `pok` for `c[0]` and `pop` for `q`, and replaces `c[1]` with `-(S + c_A[0] + c_A[2] x_v^2) / x_v`, where `S = sum over the four honest commitments of F_k(x_v)` and `x_v` is the victim's address-derived identifier. `keyGenCommit` checks only `c.length`, the PoK and the PoP, so it is accepted and completes the round.
3. Every validator accepts the commitment and computes its share. The victim's validator submits `keyGenSecretShare` with `y = (0, 0)` (fixed gas, no simulation), which reverts in `FROSTParticipantMap.set`. The attacker never calls `keyGenSecretShare`.
4. Nobody complains (no encrypted share from the attacker exists). At `last commitment block + 120`, `shared` holds the three other honest validators, `excluded = {attacker, victim}`, `participants_set` returns `None`, the rollover state becomes `EpochSkipped`.
5. 1,440 blocks later the next rollover starts with everyone included; the attacker repeats.

The genesis variant only stalls the genesis ceremony (no deadline), which is A16 and Informational.

## Considered and rejected

- The attacker cannot avoid being caught if it shares: the cancelling coefficient has an unknown discrete log, so its shares fail VSS verification and threshold complaints restart without it alone (`state/keygen.rs:735-743`). That is why the attack needs the withheld share, and the timeout path is the defect.
- The PoK cannot be bypassed and the group key cannot be cancelled: `c[0]` needs a known discrete log, checked onchain (`FROSTCoordinator.sol:374`) and offchain (`frost/keygen.rs:96-100`).
- Forcing `Y_v` to a non-identity point the contract mishandles (for example `x >= N`, which `mulmuladd` refuses) is not worse: `set` accepts any on-curve `y`, the victim is in `shared`, and only the attacker is excluded.
- Order of events: a slow honest committer after the attacker breaks the cancellation (its term is random), so the attacker simply waits for all four; the 120-block window is ample.
- Settled and known lists (`settled.md`): genesis-only liveness (A16) is excluded and kept only as a variant; the known ECDH pad item and issue #69 (one dishonest participant removed per failed keygen) do not cover a rule that removes an honest participant together with the attacker.

## Remediation options

1. When the commitment round completes (`finalize_key_gen_commitments`), compute `VerifyingShare::from_commitment` for every participant; if any is the identity, restart at once excluding the participant whose commitment completed the round (the last `KeyGenCommitted` in chain order). An honest final commitment is independent of the others and makes every `Y_i` uniform, so only a commitment chosen after all others can force the identity; the rule is deterministic for every validator and sound with several colluders, since the final term must be theirs. Recommended.
2. At the share deadline, do not exclude a participant whose public verifying share is the identity (computable from `GroupCommitments`), since the contract makes its share impossible; exclude the remaining non-sharers. Smaller change, but it wastes a share round and leaves the doomed group onchain.
3. A contract change is not a fix on its own: `getKey` and `mulmuladd` reject identity keys, so a participant with an identity verifying share could never sign.

Tests to add: a state-machine test with 5 validators where the last committer cancels one honest verifying share and withholds its share, asserting a restart that excludes only the attacker; replace the premise of `accepts_cancelled_public_key_shares` with that expectation. No code is committed.

Confirmation and notes:

- The withheld share is essential, and this file states it correctly. If the attacker publishes its (necessarily invalid) shares, the honest plaintiffs reach the complaint threshold and the restart excludes the accused alone (`state/keygen.rs:735-743`), so the victim is back in the next group.
- One commitment can cancel up to `t - 1` verifying shares, since the `t - 1` non-constant coefficients are free points (two victims at threshold 3). For Aegis one victim already skips the epoch.
- Remediation 3's caveat holds: `getKey` rejects a zero key (`FROSTParticipantMap.sol:252-255`), so a stored identity `y` makes `FROST.verifyShare` revert for that member at every signing (`FROSTCoordinator.sol:568`). The team's open item from the review of #1025 names "a contract 'has shared' flag so `y = (0, 0)` can be stored" as its preferred fix; on its own that lets the key generation finish but leaves the victim unable to sign for the epoch. Remediation 1 is sound: an honest final commitment is uniform and independent of the earlier ones, so only a commitment chosen after all others can force an identity verifying share, and blaming the participant whose commitment completed the round is deterministic on each branch.
- Spec claims contradicted: the #1025 squash message (`7fc1e08`) says the victim "publishes `y = (0,0)`, which the contract accepts", and the test comment at `frost/mod.rs:305-306` assumes the same. `Secp256k1.sol:194-196` contradicts both.
- Known status: not in `settled.md`. The team's PR review holds the same defect as an open item from the review of #1025 (Medium; filing as a public issue or advisory is remedcu's call). It is open. Reconciliation should link the two.

QA recipe: E1 already covers the cryptographic and contract halves (`L-crypto-identity-share.txt`). For the glue, add a transition test in a scratch copy of `crates/validator/src/state/keygen.rs`, as R6 did (`rust-audit/state/run3/logs/R6-keygen-warp-order.txt`): one honest `Transition` fed the five commitments of the L-crypto vectors (attacker last), the three other honest `KeyGenSecretShared` events (no victim share, no attacker share), then `NewBlock` at its share deadline. ## QA second reproduction (QA3-2A, 2026-10-09)

Result: Reproduced.

Route: a different attacker position from the one-victim case. The last committer cancels TWO honest verifying shares at once (threshold 3 leaves c[1] and c[2] free, the Critic's "up to t-1 victims" note). The on-chain rejection of y=(0,0) with Secp256k1.NotOnCurve (why the two victim shares never land, hence never enter `shared`) is L-crypto's executed E1; here the two victim share events are simply not delivered, as on chain.

Command and output: poc/F3-XC-100/repro-2/ (qa2a_xc100_two_victims.rs, RUN.md, baseline.out). `cargo test --offline -p validator --bins qa2a_xc100 -- --nocapture`.

Bite check: remediation option 1 applied in handle_key_gen_committed (detect an identity verifying share when the round completes, via a zero share that verify_secret_share accepts, and restart excluding the last committer). The poisoned group then never reaches share collection; the validator restarts into a fresh group excluding only the attacker (4 of 5 remain, viable), so the PoC's "poisoned group proceeds to CollectingShares" assertion fails (with-fix.out). Remediation sound.
