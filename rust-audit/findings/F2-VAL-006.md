## Claim

Any registered participant can file one complaint against every other participant (contract) and the validator answers each complaint against itself by publishing the plaintext share `f_self(plaintiff)` with no plausibility check, no per-plaintiff budget and no record of who complained. A single malicious member therefore forces `n-1` plaintext evaluations (one point of every honest polynomial) onto the chain and `n-1` response transactions, at the cost of `n-1` cheap `keyGenComplain` calls; `m` colluding members publish `m` points of every honest polynomial. Since `m < t` this does not reconstruct a polynomial on its own, but the protocol offers no accountability: the plaintiff's own `complaints` counter is cleared by each response, it can still confirm, and nothing in the Rust state (`Complaint { total, unresponded }` is keyed by accused only) remembers that it complained. The only liveness guard is that every honest accused must land its response before `deadlines.response` or be excluded, which turns a burst of complaints into a fee-market race for the honest side.

Two smaller parity points: the Rust ignores the `compromised` field of `KeyGenComplained` and counts _all_ complaints against an accused (`total`), while the contract counts _unresolved_ ones (`accusations` is decremented on `respond`), so the Rust restarts a round in cases where the contract still considers the group healthy; because the Rust is the stricter side and all honest nodes agree, this is a documentation/consistency issue rather than a divergence. This finding is the amplifier that F2-VAL-001 relies on; it is filed separately because the mitigations are independent.

## Trigger

Member `M` calls `keyGenComplain(gid, X)` for every other member `X` in one block during `SHARING` or before `deadlines.complain`. Each honest `X` queues `keyGenComplaintResponse(gid, M, f_X(M))` (row 2), publishing one evaluation of `f_X`; `X` must land it before `deadlines.response` (row 5). `M`'s counters are cleared by the responses (row 4) and `M` confirms normally. Nothing records that `M` was the plaintiff of `n-1` baseless complaints, so `M` repeats this in every key generation.

## Considered and rejected

- _The per-accused threshold bounds the leak._ It bounds the leak per polynomial to `t-1` points across _all_ plaintiffs, which is the designed safety bound; it does not bound what one plaintiff can force, nor deter it.
- _The plaintiff cannot confirm with open complaints, so it is punished._ The complaints are answered (row 2, row 4), which re-enables confirmation; the only "punishment" is the case where the accused fails to respond, which excludes the accused (row 5), not the plaintiff.
- _H9 from the analysis (late complaint excludes the plaintiff)._ Correct as far as it goes, but a member can waste a round equally well by simply not confirming; the late-complaint path (`state/keygen.rs:692-695`) gives it no additional power and is not filed.

## Remediation options

1. Bound plaintext responses per plaintiff and per round (e.g. answer at most one complaint from the same plaintiff per key generation, or none once the plaintiff has been answered by `k` distinct accused), and treat a plaintiff whose complaints were all answered validly as misbehaving in the local exclusion policy for the _next_ attempt. Tradeoff: a policy decision; needs the plaintiff recorded in state.
2. Change the response to a verifiable non-revealing form (publish the pairwise ECDH point with a DLEQ proof against `q_self`, letting anyone re-derive the ciphertext the plaintiff received), so complaints cost the plaintiff its own privacy rather than the accused's. Tradeoff: protocol and contract change; also closes F2-VAL-001's amplifier.
3. Read `compromised` from the event and document why the local count intentionally differs from the contract's unresolved count.

Tests to add: a state test filing `n-1` complaints from one plaintiff and asserting the number of `KeyGenComplaintResponse` commands (currently `n-1`).

## Critic (C2-VAL-A)

**Finding verdict: Confirmed. Severity Low (unchanged). Certainty 70%.**

All Basis rows Supported against re-opened code: `Complaint { total, unresponded }` is keyed by accused only, with no plaintiff record (state/mod.rs:225-232); every `accused == self` complaint is answered unconditionally with the plaintext `f_self(plaintiff)` (keygen.rs:734-744; reveal at frost/keygen.rs:420-427); `total` counts all complaints and `compromised` is never read (keygen.rs:714-722; bindings.rs:192); the contract allows one complaint per pair, `n-1` per plaintiff, clears the plaintiff's counter on each response and lets it confirm at zero (`FROSTParticipantMap.sol:183, 189-208, 222`); an unresponded complaint past `deadlines.response` excludes the _accused_, never the plaintiff (keygen.rs:1078-1086). So a single member forces `n-1` plaintext polynomial-point reveals and `n-1` honest response txs at the cost of `n-1` cheap calls, with no accountability, repeatable every ceremony.

Severity **Low** stands on its own merits: `m < t` colluders learn only `m` points of each honest polynomial, below reconstruction, so standalone this is griefing / a fee-market race on the honest side / an accountability gap, not share recovery. Its real weight is as the **amplifier of the Critical F2-VAL-001** (there the copied `q` makes each forced reveal yield a pad), correctly filed separately since the mitigations are independent. I checked the "Rust stricter than the contract" parity point: `total >= threshold` needs `threshold` _distinct_ plaintiffs against one accused (one complaint per pair onchain), i.e. a majority, so a minority cannot weaponise the stricter local restart — it is a documentation/consistency note, not an exploitable divergence, and the honest nodes coordinate any restart via commitments to the new gid, so no consensus split. Remediations 1-2 (bound per-plaintiff, or a non-revealing DLEQ response) are sound. No promotion.

With this validator in `CollectingShares` holding a real `SharingState`, feeding one `KeyGenComplained{accused: self, plaintiff: PEER1, compromised: false}` yields **exactly one** `Action::KeyGenComplaintResponse` whose `secret_share` equals `reveal_secret_share(sharing_state, PEER1)` — the plaintext polynomial evaluation — with no plausibility check and the `compromised` field ignored (Basis 2-3). The PoC then asserts the resulting `complaints` map has an entry keyed by the **accused** (`total == 1`) and **none** keyed by the plaintiff, i.e. the plaintiff is never recorded and pays nothing (Basis 1). Not single-node-reproducible: the network-aggregate "one plaintiff forces `n-1` reveals" needs `n-1` honest accused nodes; each honest node's local behaviour (unconditional reveal, no accountability) is what the PoC executes, and the aggregate follows directly.

**Remediation check.** Option 1 (bound plaintext responses per plaintiff and record the plaintiff so a serial false plaintiff is penalised next attempt) is **sound**: since the contract already allows only one complaint per `(plaintiff, accused)` pair (`FROSTParticipantMap.sol:183`), capping at one response per plaintiff does not break a genuinely-wronged plaintiff's ability to recover its share. It needs the plaintiff carried in `Complaint` state — a snapshot addition, not a transition-purity change, so it respects the core runtime contract. Option 2 (non-revealing DLEQ response) is heavier (protocol + contract change) but independently closes F2-VAL-001's amplifier. Option 3 (read `compromised`, document the intentional local/contract count divergence) is a partial hardening. At least one option is sound.
