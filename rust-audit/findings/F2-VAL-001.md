## Claim

Nothing binds the DKG encryption public key `q` published in `KeyGenCommitted` to the participant that publishes it. The Rust side decodes `q`, rejects only the identity point and verifies the FROST proof of knowledge over `c[0]` only; the contract checks only that `q` is a non-zero curve point. A malicious member `M` can therefore wait for an honest member `A` to commit and then commit with its own valid polynomial and proof of knowledge but with `q_M := q_A`.

Because the share pad is the raw, symmetric ECDH x-coordinate (`pad(X, Y) = x(sk_X · q_Y) = x(sk_Y · q_X)`, see F2-VAL-002), every honest peer `B_j` now encrypts both `f_{B_j}(M)` (to `M`) and `f_{B_j}(A)` (to `A`) under the identical pad `x(sk_{B_j} · sk_A · G)`, and `A` encrypts `f_A(B_j)` under that same pad. `M` cannot compute these pads, but it can make every honest peer publish one plaintext under each of them: the validator answers any `KeyGenComplained{accused: self}` by publishing `f_self(plaintiff)` in the clear, with no check of the plaintiff's claim, and the contract lets a registered plaintiff file one complaint against each other participant. After `M` complains once against every peer, `pad(B_j, A) = c_{B_j→M} ⊕ f_{B_j}(M)` is public for every `j`, which decrypts `f_{B_j}(A)` and `f_A(B_j)` for every `j`. With `n-1 ≥ t` evaluations of `f_A` known, `M` interpolates `f_A` entirely (hence `f_A(A)` and `a_0^A`) and computes `s_A = f_A(A) + f_M(A) + Σ_j f_{B_j}(A)`: honest member `A`'s complete long-lived signing share.

The complaint budget is counted per accused only (`total >= threshold` restarts the round), so `n-1` complaints spread over `n-1` distinct accused never trip it; once the responses land, `M` can encrypt its own shares correctly with the learned pads, nobody complains against `M`, `M`'s open-complaint counter is back to zero so it can confirm, and the group finalises with `M` holding two shares. `m` colluders each copying a distinct honest `q` hold `2m` shares; for `n = 7` two colluders (below the one-third fault bound) hold `4 ≥ t = 4` shares and can forge group signatures, i.e. attest arbitrary Safe transactions or roll the epoch over to a set of their choosing. A plain equality check on `q` would not close this: `q_M = q_A + G` or `k·q_A` leaks the pad up to sign, which two trial decryptions against the public commitment resolve.

## Trigger

Group of `n ≥ 4` (default genesis/testnet sizes are 4 and 6, `consensus/group.rs:452-474, 478-558`), honest members `A, B_1..B_{n-2}`, one malicious member `M` in the configured participant set.

1. `A` publishes `KeyGenCommitted{q_A, c_A, r_A, mu_A}`. `M` runs `dkg::part1` honestly for its own polynomial and PoK, but calls `keyGenCommit` with `q := q_A`. `verify_commitment` (row 1-2) and the contract (row 3) accept it.
2. Commitments complete. Every honest node runs `generate_secret_shares` (row 5): `B_j` publishes `c_{B_j→M} = f_{B_j}(M) ⊕ P_j` and `c_{B_j→A} = f_{B_j}(A) ⊕ P_j` with `P_j = x(sk_{B_j}·sk_A·G)`; `A` publishes `c_{A→B_j} = f_A(B_j) ⊕ P_j` and `c_{A→M} = f_A(M) ⊕ x(sk_A²·G)`.
3. `M` calls `keyGenComplain(gid, B_j)` for every `j` and `keyGenComplain(gid, A)` (allowed in `SHARING`, row 10-12). Each honest node, seeing `accused == self`, queues `keyGenComplaintResponse(gid, M, f_self(M))` (rows 8-9). Per-accused totals stay at 1 (row 7); no restart.
4. From the public events, `M` computes `P_j = c_{B_j→M} ⊕ f_{B_j}(M)`, then `f_{B_j}(A) = c_{B_j→A} ⊕ P_j` and `f_A(B_j) = c_{A→B_j} ⊕ P_j` for all `j`, and `x(sk_A²·G) = c_{A→M} ⊕ f_A(M)`.
5. `M` now knows `f_A` at the `n-1` points `{B_j} ∪ {M}` (`≥ t`, row 14), interpolates `f_A`, and computes `s_A = f_A(A) + f_M(A) + Σ_j f_{B_j}(A)` (all terms known: `f_M(A)` is its own).
6. `M` publishes `keyGenSecretShare` with `y_M = Φ(id_M)` and `f_M(B_j) ⊕ P_j`, `f_M(A) ⊕ x(sk_A²·G)`: every recipient decrypts a valid share, nobody complains against `M`. Responses have cleared `M`'s plaintiff counter, so `M` confirms; all confirm; the group is `FINALIZED` with `M` a member holding `s_M` and `s_A`.

The `keyGenComplain`/`keyGenComplaintResponse` round trip must fit the share-round deadline (`key_gen_timeout`, default 120 blocks) - roughly ten minutes on Gnosis - which is ample for `n-1` transactions.

## Considered and rejected

- _The PoK covers `q`._ It does not: `verify_proof_of_knowledge` (frost-core-3.0.0/src/keys/dkg.rs:445-464, read this session) is a Schnorr proof over `commitment.verifying_key()`, i.e. `c[0]`; `q` is a separate scalar sampled in `EncryptionKey::generate` (`frost/ecdh.rs:29-36`) and only serialised next to the package in `marshal::solidity_commitment` (`frost/marshal.rs:25-38`).
- _`M` cannot produce valid shares without `sk_A`, so the round would restart against it._ True before step 3; after step 3-4 `M` knows every pad it needs (symmetry, row 4). Honest recipients' `verify_encrypted_secret_share` (`frost/keygen.rs:337-390`) then passes for `M`'s shares.
- _The per-accused threshold or the contract's `compromised` flag stops it._ Both are per accused (rows 7, 12); `M` files at most one complaint per accused.
- _A late complaint is ignored (`state/keygen.rs:692-695`)._ The attack files complaints during `CollectingShares`, which has no deadline gate (row 10).
- _The honest accused could refuse to answer._ Refusing is not implemented (row 8), and an unanswered complaint gets the accused excluded at `deadlines.response` (`state/keygen.rs:1083-1086`), so honest nodes must answer.
- _A duplicate-`q` check would fix it._ It would raise the bar only marginally: with `q_M = q_A + G`, a revealed `f_{B_j}(M)` gives `x(P_j + q_{B_j})` and hence `P_j` up to sign; two trial decryptions of `c_{B_j→A}` against `Φ_{B_j}(A)` (`frost/keygen.rs:316-324`) pick the right one. A proof of possession or a pad bound to the direction is required (see remediation).
- _Genesis only (A16)._ No: the same code runs for every rollover key generation (`state/keygen.rs:473-507, 912-981`).

## Remediation options

1. Require a proof of possession for `q`: include `q` in the DKG challenge preimage (a second Schnorr proof, or fold `q` into `HDKG(id, c[0], R, q)` in both `frost/keygen.rs::verify_commitment` and `FROST.keyGenChallenge`), and reject commitments whose proof does not verify. Tradeoff: a small ABI change to `KeyGenCommitment` and a contract change; closes the copy/related-key vector at the source.
2. Derive the pad with a KDF bound to the pair and direction, e.g. `HKDF-SHA256(x(shared), info = gid ‖ sender ‖ receiver)` (safenet-core already ships `kdf::derive_key`), so that a copied `q` yields pads that are useless in the other direction. Tradeoff: a wire-format change for `f`; also fixes F2-VAL-002. Best combined with option 1.
3. Redesign the complaint response so that it does not publish share plaintext on demand (e.g. reveal the pairwise ECDH point with a DLEQ proof, which lets everyone re-derive the plaintiff's ciphertext without learning other pads), and bound complaints per plaintiff. Tradeoff: protocol change; independently valuable (see F2-VAL-006).

Tests to add: a `frost::keygen` unit test with three parties where party 3 replaces its `encryption_key` public key by party 1's, runs `generate_secret_shares` for all three, and asserts that `reveal_secret_share(party2, party3) ⊕ c_{2→3} ⊕ c_{2→1}` decodes to a scalar satisfying `verify` against party 2's commitment at party 1's identifier; a state-machine flow test (the epic's 7B phase) that a complaint-only strategy finalises a group with the copier as a member.

**Remediation check (as requested).** Option 1 (proof of possession for `q`, folding `q` into the DKG challenge preimage `HDKG(id, c[0], R, q)`) is **sound and closes the mechanism at its source**: a member can no longer publish a `q` it does not control. Neither breaks the core runtime contract (`crates/core/src/state/mod.rs`): both are crypto/ABI changes, not transition-purity changes. Recommended: adopt 1 **and** 2.
