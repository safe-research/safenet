## Claim

`frost::keygen::verify_commitment` is the validator's only gate between an onchain `KeyGenCommitted` event body and `frost-core`. It performs exactly one check — the proof of knowledge — and states in a comment that the structural checks are somebody else's job:

> `// Note that we do not check the length of the commitments, this is enforced` `// by the smart contract and any issues will be caught later and produce an` `// unexpected FROST error.`

Two things are wrong with that division of labour.

**First, the contract is not in the path.** The comment names `FROSTCoordinator`'s `require(commitment.c.length == state.threshold, InvalidGroupCommitment)` as the enforcer, and under A7 that is true of a commitment the coordinator accepted. But the validator does not require the event to have come from the coordinator (F-VAL-060). Once one allow-listed `oracles` address can emit a `KeyGenCommitted` log, `c` arrives with a length and a content nobody has checked, and `marshal::frost_commitment` will build a `VerifiableSecretSharingCommitment` out of whatever vector it is given — including the empty one.

**Second, the fallback the comment relies on is unreadable in this run.** "Any issues will be caught later and produce an unexpected FROST error" is an assertion about `frost-core` 3.0.0 internals. No dependency source is on disk (`state/baseline.md` §1, assumption A6), so whether `dkg::verify_proof_of_knowledge` on a zero-length commitment returns `Err`, indexes `[0]` and panics, or silently succeeds cannot be established here. A panic in the state-machine transition is not a caught error — the driver has no catch-unwind anywhere on that path.

Separately, `marshal::frost_point` maps the ABI value `(0, 0)` to the curve identity by design (`marshal.rs:136-138`) and `frost_commitment` passes each such point straight into `CoefficientCommitment::new` without an `is_identity` check — while the sibling decoder `frost_signing_commitments` _does_ reject the identity for `d` and `e` (`marshal.rs:168-170`), and `EncryptionPublicKey::from_point` rejects it for `q` (`ecdh.rs:70-75`). The polynomial commitment vector is the one place in this module where a degenerate point is accepted. A participant whose `c[0]` is the identity contributes `a_0 = 0` and satisfies the proof of knowledge trivially, since the verification equation degenerates to `R = g^mu` for any `mu` the prover chooses.

## Trigger

None identified that is independent of F-VAL-060. With F-VAL-060's precondition (one allow-listed `oracles` address able to emit a Coordinator-shaped log), a `KeyGenCommitted` event carrying `c = []` or `c = [(0,0), ...]` reaches `verify_commitment` with the contract's `require` never having run. What happens next is `frost-core`'s to decide and is unreadable here — which is precisely the finding: the code's stated safety argument ("caught later") is unverified, and the code path that was supposed to make it moot is bypassable.

Without F-VAL-060 the identity-coefficient half still stands on its own but is not attacker-reachable: `Secp256k1.requireNonZero` guards `q` onchain, and nothing onchain rejects an identity `c[i]` either, so a _registered_ participant can publish `c[0] = (0,0)` today. Its effect is a zero contribution to the group key with a trivially forgeable proof of knowledge, which biases nothing an adversary controls and is therefore Informational on its own.

## Considered and rejected

- **"`VerifiableSecretSharingCommitment::new` validates."** Cannot be asserted: it is `frost-core` 3.0.0 and the source is not on disk (A6). Any claim either way is class `I`. That is the point.
- **"An empty `c` cannot reach here because the ABI decoder requires a non-empty array."** No. `c` is a `Point[]` in the generated bindings; alloy decodes a zero-length dynamic array without error, and `frost_commitment`'s `.map(...).collect` over an empty iterator yields an empty `Vec`.
- **"The proof of knowledge would fail for a degenerate commitment."** For an _identity_ `c[0]` the verification equation degenerates rather than fails — that is the concern, not an aside. For an _empty_ `c` there is no `c[0]` to verify against at all, which is the unreadable case.
- **"This duplicates F-VAL-001."** It does not. F-VAL-001 is about `q` having no proof of possession and explicitly notes that "the check `verify_commitment` does covers the polynomial commitment vector `c`, **not** `q`" (F-VAL-001, Claim). This finding is the mirror: the check that _does_ cover `c` is a proof of knowledge only, and the structural validation of `c` is delegated out of the Rust entirely.
- **"R4 already refuted this."** R4 recorded it as Observations O3 and O4 and marked O4 "Refuted as reachable harm ... Becomes reachable only if Coordinator-shaped events can be injected from an address that is not the coordinator (VAL-H2, R6's finding)." R6 filed VAL-H2 as F-VAL-060. The condition R4 attached is satisfied and the observation was never revisited.

## Remediation options

1. **Validate `c` where it is decoded.** In `frost_commitment`, reject an empty `c`, reject any `is_identity` coefficient, and reject a `c` whose length does not equal the caller-supplied threshold. Three lines; makes the module self-sufficient and removes the dependence on both the contract and on `frost-core`'s undocumented tolerance. Tradeoff: `verify_commitment` needs the threshold as a parameter — the caller already has it (`group.size` at `state/keygen.rs:171`).
2. **Bind events to their emitter** (F-VAL-060 option 1). This restores the comment's premise rather than removing the dependence on it. Cheaper, but it leaves the Rust with no defence in depth and leaves the `frost-core` question open for any future non-contract caller of this `pub fn`.
3. **Delete the comment's second clause.** "Any issues will be caught later and produce an unexpected FROST error" is an unverified claim about an upstream crate sitting in a security-critical decoder. Either prove it with a test or stop asserting it. A negative test that feeds `c = vec![]` and `c = vec![Point::ZERO; t]` to `verify_commitment` settles it in one run and is the cheapest way to close basis row 8.

Tests to add: `verify_commitment` negative cases for an empty `c`, an identity `c[0]`, and a `c` whose length is not the threshold — the third of which currently has no assertion anywhere in the workspace. No code is committed.

### Correction 1 — the title is wrong; the contract _is_ in the event path

"delegates ... to a contract that is not in the event path" is the load-bearing framing and it is false in normal operation. In `FROSTCoordinator.keyGenCommit` the length check runs at `:378` and the event is emitted at `:382`, in the same call:

```solidity
Secp256k1.requireNonZero(commitment.q);
require(commitment.c.length == state.threshold, InvalidGroupCommitment);
group.participants.register(msg.sender, poap);
group.state = state;
group.key = Secp256k1.add(group.key, commitment.c[0]);
emit KeyGenCommitted(gid, msg.sender, commitment, committed);
```

The coordinator is not some other contract off the path — it is the emitter, and the `require` strictly precedes the `emit`. A `KeyGenCommitted` event from the coordinator _cannot_ carry a `c` of the wrong length. The body concedes this ("under A7 that is true") and conditions on F-VAL-060, so the argument is honest; the **title asserts the unconditional version** and should be reworded to "...to a contract that is bypassed if events are not bound to their emitter". As written it will be read as a standalone defect, which it is not.

Consequently the empty-`c` half inherits F-VAL-060's precondition (50%, and ~25% by F-VAL-060's own reviewer) _and_ rests on an `I`-class core — what `frost-core` does with an empty commitment vector. Two conditionals stacked on an unreadable base. The finding is right that this is uncomfortable and right that the code's own safety argument is unverifiable here; it is not right that anything has been shown to break.

### Correction 2 — the identity half is real, reachable today, and I can bound it precisely

This is the part worth keeping, and it needs no F-VAL-060. I traced the onchain side, which neither R4 (Observation O3) nor this file established:

- `commitment.c[i]` gets **no** `Secp256k1.requireNonZero`. The only check on `c[0]` is that `Secp256k1.add` consumes it (`FROSTCoordinator.sol:381`), and `add` explicitly handles the point at infinity (`Secp256k1.sol:83-88`, `"P is the point at infinity, and 0 + Q = Q"`), while its `_unpack` deliberately admits the zero point (`Secp256k1.sol:210-213`, `valid := or(iszero(or(x, y)), valid)`). So `c[0] = (0,0)` **is registrable onchain today** by a legitimately registered participant. The finding's Trigger section says this and is correct.
- But the fully degenerate case is blocked. A participant whose whole `c` is identity has `f ≡ 0`, hence `y = identity = (0,0)`, and `FROSTParticipantMap.set` calls `y.requireNonZero` (`:162`), which is the _strict_ guard `require(_satisfiesCurveEquation(p.x, p.y))` (`Secp256k1.sol:178-180`) — and `(0,0)` fails it, since `0 != 7`. The share round reverts. Only `c[0] = identity` with non-identity higher coefficients survives to the end of the ceremony.

So the exploit surface is exactly: one participant contributes `a_0 = 0` to the group secret. With at least one honest contributor the group key remains uniform; the attacker cannot _choose_ it, because the proof of knowledge forces it to know its own `dlog` and it cannot know `Σ_j C_j[0]`'s. What remains is ordinary last-committer bias, a documented and accepted property of Pedersen-style DKG, plus an asymmetry: two sibling decoders in the same module reject the identity and this one does not. That is a hardening gap, not a break. **Informational-to-Low on its own** — which is what the finding itself concludes, and I agree.

### Correction 3 — no interaction with F-VAL-001 (the coordinator's specific question)

The intuition that "an identity coefficient is a classic way to force a known key share" applies to schemes where the commitment **is** the key. That is precisely the design `docs/overview.md:48` describes — `C[0]` abused as the ECDH public key — and precisely the design this code **abandoned** in favour of a separate `q` (see my F-VAL-002 critique, which treats that substitution as the root cause of F-VAL-001). Because `q` is a separate field and `EncryptionPublicKey::from_point` rejects the identity outright (`ecdh.rs:70-75`, claim 6 here), an identity `c[0]` can never become an ECDH key and buys an F-VAL-001 attacker nothing. Conversely F-VAL-001's copied `q` is a valid non-identity point that gains nothing from a degenerate `c`. **The two are independent**; neither subsumes nor cheapens the other. The one thing they share is the file, and the fact that `verify_commitment` is thin in both directions.

The finding's own "This duplicates F-VAL-001 — it does not" bullet reaches the same conclusion by a different route and is correct.

### Finding verdict

**Plausible. Certainty 42%. Severity Medium / Low.**

Plausible: the code facts are all `E2` and re-verified — the delegated length check, the absent identity test, the sibling decoders that do have one. But the finding's own Trigger section says "None identified that is independent of F-VAL-060" for the serious half, and the half that _is_ independent (identity `c[0]`) has a bounded, non-exploitable effect that I pinned down above. That is mechanism-verified, trigger-unproven.

Certainty 42%, just inside the band. The empty-`c` half is a conditional stacked on an `I`-class unknown and I decline to carry it higher; the identity half is certain as a code fact but its _harm_ is what is unproven, and a finding is rated on the defect it claims. If a QA agent establishes that `frost-core` panics rather than errors on an empty commitment vector, this goes straight to Confirmed and to High — that single unreadable fact is doing all the work.

Severity Low, down from Medium: no attacker-reachable harm has been demonstrated. The identity-coefficient gap is a real inconsistency worth closing, and the fix is cheap, but by the brief's scale ("a panic that no untrusted input can reach is Low or Informational however alarming it looks") this does not clear Medium on present evidence.

**Remediation note.** Option 1 is the right fix and is worth applying regardless of the verdict: rejecting an empty `c`, rejecting `is_identity` coefficients, and checking `|c| == threshold` inside `frost_commitment` is three lines, makes the module self-sufficient, removes the dependence on both the contract and on `frost-core`'s undocumented tolerance, and closes the asymmetry with `frost_signing_commitments` in the same file. It also sits naturally alongside F-VAL-001's option 2 (a proof of possession for `q`), since both harden the same decode site. I would ship them together.

**To settle this**, QA needs one thing above all: run `verify_commitment` against a `KeyGenCommitment` with `c = []` and with `c[0] = (0,0)` and record what `frost-core` actually does. That is a unit test with no chain and no Anvil, it resolves claim 8 — the only `I` in the file — and it decides between Low and High in a single run.
