## Claim

Only the genesis-to-epoch-1 key generation opens on a log: the handler for the final genesis `KeyGenConfirmed` finalizes genesis and calls `start_key_gen` for epoch 1 with `deadline = block + key_gen_timeout` (`keygen.rs:470-500`). Every later round opens on a block (`handle_rollover_new_block`, `keygen.rs:969-979`). `handle_key_gen_committed` accepts a commitment only while the state is `CollectingCommitments` for the event's group and drops anything else (`_ => (state, Vec::new())`, `keygen.rs:262-263`).

Onchain, `keyGenCommit` accepts the epoch-1 commitment whenever that group is `COMMITTING`, with no dependency on the genesis confirmations (`FROSTCoordinator.sol:361-379`), and a second commitment from the same address reverts in `register` (`FROSTParticipantMap.sol:148`, `require(state.status == ParticipantStatus.NONE, InvalidParticipant())`).

In live order a validator sends its epoch-1 commitment only after it has processed the final genesis confirmation, so the commitment always lands later. A reorg that removes both transactions can re-include them in the other order: they come from two senders, so no nonce orders them. Every validator then processes the participant's commitment while genesis is still in `CollectingConfirmations` and drops it. The round opens on the re-included confirmation. When the participant's setup resumes, its own commitment is missing from `commitments`, so it publishes the commitment again (`keygen.rs:91-106`), and that transaction reverts onchain. At the deadline the round restarts without the participant (`exclude_all_others(commitments.keys())`, `keygen.rs:1042-1061`, restart at `:1196-1240`). The participant sits out epoch 1 as an observer and rejoins at the next rollover, which recomputes the set with an empty exclusion list (`keygen.rs:969-979`). If enough commitments are reordered that the remaining set cannot be formed, the restart fails with `could not form new participant set to restart keygen` and epoch 1 is skipped, which is what the two-validator CA3-FIX run showed.

## Trigger

1. Genesis completes: four of five `KeyGenConfirmed` are mined, then the fifth at block B. Every validator opens the epoch-1 round at B and sends its commitment, which lands at B + 1 or later.
2. Within `max_reorg_depth` (Aegis 5), a reorg of at least two blocks removes the final genesis confirmation and at least one participant's epoch-1 commitment.
3. The new canonical chain includes that commitment before the final confirmation (an earlier block, or a lower log index in the same block).
4. Every validator drops the commitment, the round reopens on the confirmation, the participant's second commitment reverts, and at `B' + key_gen_timeout` (Aegis 120 blocks, 10 min) the round restarts without it.

This window exists once per deployment, at the end of the genesis ceremony.

## Considered and rejected

- A16 and settled.md: genesis completes normally; the loss is in the epoch-1 rollover key generation. A16's narrow reading keeps "rollover key generations, epoch-1 loss" in scope at full severity, and settled.md section 1 says anything reaching a rollover keeps its severity. Not `known`.
- F2-VAL-003 (Fixed by #1017): same consequence, but through a block-opened round and a warp that delivered logs before the block tick. Here the round opens on a log and the reorder comes from a reorg, which #1017 does not touch.
- F3-VAL-062: a restart drops peer secret shares while `Resume::Setup` is outstanding. Different event and different trigger.
- F-VAL-004 (rollover instance): a lost `KeyGenSetup` effect. Different mechanism.
- F-XC-050: an injected `KeyGenConfirmed` closes the round early. Different mechanism.
- F-VAL-005 (Fixed): the secrets are kept across the reorg and the commitment is byte-identical (CA3-FIX). The loss here is the dropped event, not a secret mismatch.
- "The participant can commit again." `register` rejects a second commitment from the same address (basis 4).
- "With every validator online it does not happen." CA3-FIX saw 0 of 3 with everyone online, because the queues happened to re-send the confirmations before the commitments. On a real chain a reorg returns both transactions to the mempool and the block producer picks the order, so nothing guarantees it.

## Remediation options

1. While genesis is in `CollectingConfirmations`, accept and keep a `KeyGenCommitted` for the next-epoch group id, which is deterministic (`participants_set(Epoch::Number { number: 1, excluded: {} })`), and seed it into `commitments` when `start_key_gen` opens that round. Tradeoff: one more field in the persisted state.
2. General form: keep commitments that arrive for a group whose round is not open yet, bounded to the expected next group, and apply them when the round opens. This also covers any future log-opened round.
3. When the setup resumes with the own commitment missing, read the participant's onchain registration through an effect and treat a registered commitment as present instead of re-publishing it. Tradeoff: an RPC read in the key-generation path.

**Bite check.** Temporary fix in `wt/lt3`: keep a commitment that arrives before its round is open, and seed it when `start_key_gen` opens that group's round (`poc/F3-VAL-100/bite-fix.diff`, a thread-local test stand-in for remediation option 2). Reverted. The direction is sound. A real fix must keep the buffer in the persisted state, not in a thread-local.

**Severity and practicality.** One honest validator sits out epoch 1 (1,440 blocks, about 2 h). The set stays live at 4 of 5, and the validator rejoins at the next rollover. The rubric lists "excluded under reorgs within `max_reorg_depth`" as High, but the precedent for one validator losing one epoch is Medium (F-VAL-004 rollover instance, F3-VAL-062). Triage Low: the window opens once per deployment and needs a reorg of at least two blocks at that moment plus an adverse order between two senders. Certainty 88: two independent executions and the mechanism confirmed in code. What I did not measure is how likely the trigger is on Gnosis.
