## Claim

The validator reacts to an approving `OracleResult` in the block it sees it: it generates and commits nonces at once, and as soon as every signer has committed (normally the next block) it burns its nonces and broadcasts a `signShareWithCallback` whose callback is `attestTransaction`. Nothing waits for the oracle result to become reorg-safe. Once broadcast, those transactions are irrevocable: the transaction queue resubmits allocated transactions after a reorg whatever their expiry, and nodes re-inject transactions from orphaned blocks.

If a reorg of 3 to 5 blocks (inside the handled `max_reorg_depth` of 5) replaces the blocks that produced the approval with a branch on which the SentinelOracle does not approve (the request freezes for arbitration or resolves denied), the validators' state rolls back to `WaitingForOracle`, but their shares from the orphaned branch still land on the canonical branch. `FROSTCoordinator.signShare` aggregates shares by selection root without looking at any oracle, the fifth share completes the group signature, and the callback runs `Consensus.attestTransaction`, which also never consults the oracle. The canonical chain then holds a valid attestation for a transaction its oracle disputed or denied, and `SafenetGuard` accepts attestations without checking the oracle (it leaves that to the validators). This breaks INV-V9 at the level of the canonical chain and the rule that arbitrated transactions are never attested.

## Trigger

Aegis parameters: Gnosis, 5 s blocks, 5 validators with threshold 3, SentinelOracle commit window 6 and reveal window 6, `signing_timeout` 6, `oracle_timeout` 24, `max_reorg_depth` 5, sentinel engine timeout 18.75 s.

1. Block T: `proposeTransaction` emits `TransactionProposed`, `Sign` (signature id S) and posts the request (`commitDeadline = T + 6`, `revealDeadline = T + 12`). All validators enter `WaitingForOracle` (`state/sign.rs:38-62`).
2. Sentinels with approving verdicts commit at about T+2 to T+4. A sentinel whose engine check runs close to its 18.75 s timeout sends a deny commit late, and it lands in block T+6 only on branch B; on branch A, blocks T+5 and T+6 were built without it (on A the late transaction lands at T+7 and reverts `CommitWindowClosed`, basis 10).
3. Branch A: the approving sentinels reveal at T+7 (`crates/sentinel/src/service.rs:420-447`); the last reveal auto-finalizes, `OracleResult(approved)` at block o = T+7 (or T+8).
4. Validators following A: at o they emit `GenerateNonces`, their `CommitNonces` land in o+1; when the fifth commitment is processed they burn their nonces and broadcast `signShareWithCallback` in slot o+2 (basis 1 to 4).
5. Branch B overtakes A from block T+6 (a reorg of 3 to 4 blocks, inside `max_reorg_depth`). On B the deny commit is in T+6, the deny reveal lands, and `finalize` freezes the request (`DisputeTriggered`, no `OracleResult`). The validators process `Uncle(T+6)`, their state returns to `WaitingForOracle`, and it later times out.
6. The five `signShareWithCallback` transactions from step 4 are rebroadcast (basis 5, 6) and included on B. Each share is valid for S, whose `Sign` (block T) is on both branches; the fifth completes the signature and the callback attests the transaction (basis 7, 8). The validators only log "received attestation on unexpected signing state" (`state/sign.rs:478-485`).

Result: `TransactionAttested` on the canonical chain for a request the SentinelOracle froze for arbitration. The same works with a deny reveal near `revealDeadline` (reorg of 3 blocks: `revealDeadline`, the finalize block, and the commitment block). No unprivileged actor controls the reorg; a sentinel can only make its own commit or reveal land near a window edge.

## Considered and rejected

- The state machine itself is reorg-correct: after `Uncle` it cannot reach `CollectSigningShares` without an approving result on the current branch (`crates/core/src/state/mod.rs:187-195`), and a restart keeps `oracle_approved` only from a share round (`state/sign.rs:656-668`). The defect is in what was already broadcast, not in the state.
- A 1- or 2-block reorg does not flip the outcome: with the commit window closed and the votes fixed by commitments, branches sharing every block up to the finalize block's parent resolve identically. The validators broadcast shares at the earliest in slot o+2, so a reorg that also changes the deciding commit or reveal needs depth 3 or more.
- The commitment-timeout path (one validator slow) moves the share broadcast to o+6, which would need a reorg deeper than 5; the fast path (all five commit in o+1) is the normal case with every validator online.
- Rollover packets are not affected the same way: `stageEpoch` re-checks the rollover window and needs a finalized group key onchain (`contracts/src/Consensus.sol:229-248, 395-397`), so the onchain call fails if the branch's DKG differs.
- Settled items checked: "arbitrated transactions are never attested" is settled as a design rule, and this finding is a mechanism that breaks that rule, not a dispute of it. Oracle fees for ignored results (known) is a different issue.
- Not a duplicate of the documented message-swap edge case (`docs/overview.md:70-83`), which concerns a reused signature id after a deeper reorg and costs liveness only.

## Remediation options

1. Act only on a reorg-safe approval: keep `WaitingForOracle` after an approving result, record its block, and emit `GenerateNonces` on the first `NewBlock(b)` with `b >= result_block + max_reorg_depth`. This needs `max_reorg_depth` in the validator transition config. Cost: about 25 s more per attestation on Gnosis; `oracle_timeout` 24 still covers the 13-block oracle round plus the wait. A reorg deeper than `max_reorg_depth` already stops the service (INV-C5).
2. Enforce the oracle onchain: `Consensus.attestTransaction` checks the oracle's resolved state for `message` through an `IOracle` view. Closes the gap for any client, needs a contract change and a new oracle interface method, and is outside the Rust scope.
3. Partial only: after a reorg that returns a session to `WaitingForOracle`, replace its unexecuted `SignShare` transactions with same-nonce cancellations. Racy against re-inclusion of transactions already seen by builders; not sufficient alone.

Tests to add: an Anvil integration test that builds branch A (approval, validator commitments and shares), reverts to a snapshot before the commit deadline, mines branch B with a deny commit, replays the validators' signed share transactions and asserts that no `TransactionAttested` appears (fails today).

Finding verdict: Confirmed. Severity: Critical to High. The effect is in Section 8's Critical class: the canonical chain, and any guard, gets a valid attestation for a request the SentinelOracle froze, denied or timed out, against `docs/transaction-lifecycle.md:3, 38, 45-46`, Charter Article I and `docs/overview.md:9`. I rate it High because the only trigger is a natural Gnosis reorg of 3 to 5 blocks whose fork point precedes the block where a deciding vote landed on one branch only. No actor within A2 can cause or steer the reorg, and a harmful outcome also needs a malicious transaction that some sentinel approved. The practicality check should set the final rating against Gnosis reorg-depth data, which I could not measure (no live RPC). Certainty: 80, E2 with Confirmed (band 70 to 89); not executed. Duplicate: canonical. L-crypto's observation O6 describes the same mechanism, found independently (I-class).

Answers to the four questions in the Critic brief:

1. Can a reorg within `max_reorg_depth` change the outcome? Yes. A commit counts only at `block <= commitDeadline` (`SentinelOracleRequests.sol:117`), a reveal only in `(commitDeadline, revealDeadline]` (`:126-127`), the last reveal finalizes in the same call (`:136`, `SentinelOracle.sol:256-258`), and `finalize` gives FROZEN when both sides revealed and TIMED_OUT when nobody did (`:172-211`). With the Aegis windows (6 and 6) the earliest approval is block T+7, and validators send shares only after processing o+1 (`state/sign.rs:287-324`; `docs/overview.md:83`). So:
   - a deny commit that lands in T+6 on the canonical branch only turns approve into FROZEN with a reorg of depth 3 (fork at T+5, blocks T+6 to T+8 replaced); a deciding commit in T+5 needs depth 4;
   - a deny reveal in T+12 on the canonical branch only, with the orphaned branch finalized at T+13, also needs depth 3;
   - depth 2 is enough for one flip the second "Considered and rejected" bullet misses: if every reveal landed in T+12 on the orphaned branch and none made T+12 on the canonical one, the canonical request times out with no votes while the shares from T+13 are already out. That contradicts the team's decision that a proposal whose vote closed with nothing revealed is never attested, and `docs/transaction-lifecycle.md:46`, but no sentinel denied the transaction, so it is the benign case;
   - depth 1 never lets a share out, since a share needs o+1 processed.

   The realistic edge is a late commit: the engine timeout is 18.75 s on Gnosis (`crates/sentinel/src/main.rs:48-65`) and the commit expires at the commit deadline (`crates/sentinel/src/service.rs:240, 248`), so a commit in T+5 or T+6 is the case to look for.

2. Are the shares from the orphaned branch re-included? Yes. The validator's own queue clears executed markers on the uncle and resends allocated rows with no expiry check (rows 5 and 6). Client mempools also re-inject transactions from dropped blocks (I, not checked for the Gnosis clients). Each share is valid on the canonical branch: the `sid` comes from the proposal block, before the fork, and `signShare` needs no onchain nonce commitment (row 7).

3. Does any onchain check bind the attestation to the oracle? No. The coordinator checks the share against the message and the group key (row 7), `Consensus.attestTransaction` checks `AlreadyAttested` and `signatureVerify` (row 8), and `SafenetGuard.checkTransaction` checks forest membership and `FROST.verify` (row 9). A team design decision makes oracle acceptance a validator-side policy on purpose.

4. What do the docs and the Charter intend? `docs/transaction-lifecycle.md:3` ("only after an approval"), `:38` (no arbitration outcome leads to an attestation), `:45` (DENIED is not signed), `:46` (no votes, not attested). Charter Article I (`safenet-charter` `6ef1aa9`, "Council and protocol boundaries"): "A transaction that enters arbitration is not eligible for validator attestation, regardless of the ruling." `docs/overview.md:9`: "No invalid transaction attestation will ever be produced." `docs/overview.md:83` accepts reorg sensitivity, but only describes a liveness case (the message swap) and calls 1-block reorgs harmless; it never mentions the oracle gate. The team's decisions that arbitrated transactions are never attested and that a proposal with nothing revealed is never attested rest on the property this breaks, and the second decision's revisit trigger ("validators can sign without an approving `OracleResult`") is met across such a reorg.

Correction that strengthens the finding and changes the remediation: the guard needs no share to be re-included. The group signature is `(R, z)` with `R = selection.r` and `z` the sum of the shares (`FROSTSignatureShares.sol:77, 92-93`), and every `z_i` is public in the share calldata, in the mempool and in the orphaned `SignShared` events (`FROSTCoordinator.sol:572`). Anyone who saw branch A can build a valid trailer, and `SafenetGuard` never reads `Consensus`. So remediation 2 (an oracle check in `Consensus.attestTransaction`) and remediation 3 do not close the guard path. Only remediation 1 does: emit nothing that leads to a share until the approval is at or below the safe block. It fits the restart path, which keeps `oracle_approved: true` only from a share round (`state/sign.rs:656-668`).

Side effect of the same root, not a separate finding: validators that burned their pair on branch A get `None` for that signature id on branch B (`secrets/store.rs:220-256`), so when the two branches' committed sets differ the ceremony splits as in F3-VAL-031.
