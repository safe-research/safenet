## Claim

Every `TransactionProposed` for the configured oracle spawns one `EngineCheck` effect into an unbounded `JoinSet`, each an outbound HTTP request with a timeout of up to ¾ of `(voting_window − 1)` block times (371 s with the sample config on Gnosis). Every verdict other than `Unknown` produces an `ApproveToken` + `Commit` pair regardless of how many bonds are already outstanding or whether the account can afford them. The transaction queue is FIFO by insertion order with 16 transactions in flight, and it silently discards any not-yet-submitted `Reveal` once `latest ≥ reveal_deadline`.

A sponsor able to pay the request fee up front can therefore (i) saturate the engine so that checks time out and the sentinel abstains on everything, in which case requests nobody votes on time out and the **full fee is refunded** to the sponsor, making the flood cost gas only; or (ii) sustain more proposals than the queue can carry, so that `Commit` rows inserted at resume time crowd out `Reveal` rows inserted at `commit_deadline + 1`, reveals expire unsubmitted, and each affected commitment is slashed `slashAmount` (if any other sentinel revealed on that request). The sentinel has no admission control, no per-block or total bond budget, and no back-pressure between the engine, the FSM and the queue.

## Trigger

(i) A sponsor submits `n` proposals per block for the duration of a commit window (each a `proposeTransaction` with the fee approved). Each sentinel opens `n` engine requests per block (claims 1–3). Once the engine's own RPC-backed checks exceed the per-request timeout, every check resolves `Unknown` and the sentinel abstains (`service.rs:176-179`). If every sentinel is saturated, each request times out and the sponsor is refunded (claim 7); the attacker's net cost is gas. Legitimate proposals in the same period receive no verdict.

(ii) A sponsor sustains more than 16 proposals per block that the engine _can_ answer. Each yields an `ApproveToken` + `Commit` row (claim 4). `Reveal` rows are inserted later (claim 6) and queue behind the backlog (claim 5). Reveals not submitted by `reveal_deadline` are discarded; where any other sentinel revealed on the same request, the commitment is slashed `slashAmount` (claim 8). No local signal precedes the loss.

## Considered and rejected

- "The commit window bounds bond exposure": only through the 16-in-flight cap on `Commit`s, which is an accidental limit and is what starves the reveals in (ii).
- "The engine is trusted (A3)": trust in its verdicts does not make its capacity infinite; the attacker controls the request rate, not the engine.
- "The fee makes flooding expensive": true only when sentinels vote; when they all abstain the fee is refunded (claim 7).

## Remediation options

1. Bound concurrency: a semaphore on `EngineCheck` (or a small worker pool) with an explicit queue depth; checks that cannot start before `commit_deadline − margin` resolve `Unknown` immediately instead of occupying a slot.
2. Prioritise reveals over commits in the queue (a priority column or a separate lane) and never let a `Commit` be submitted when its matching `Reveal` could not also be submitted before `reveal_deadline` at the current throughput.
3. Add a configurable budget: maximum outstanding bonds (count and token amount) and maximum commits per block; skip requests beyond it with a metric.

Tests to add: queue test with 20 `Commit` rows followed by 20 `Reveal` rows and a short reveal window asserting reveals are not dropped; effect-manager test with a concurrency cap.

Claim 10 correctly `I`.

**Independent assessment — who can flood and at what cost.** Anyone holding the fee token can call `proposeTransaction`; the only per-request cost is `fee` (refunded on `TIMED_OUT`, otherwise paid out to revealing sentinels) plus gas, which includes the FROST `sign` request the call makes (`Consensus.sol:265`). The sentinel forwards every proposal to the engine with no admission control and bonds on every verdict with no budget — mechanism verified. The two outcomes differ in how well they are shown:

- (i) _Abstention_: the fee is refunded only when **no** sentinel reveals; a single sentinel whose engine keeps up turns each flood request into a paid fee. Whether the flood saturates the engine is engine capacity — out of scope — so this branch is `I` from the sentinel's side; what is in scope is that the sentinel offers the engine no back-pressure and drops every `Unknown` permanently (`176-179`). Impact if it works: legitimate requests time out too (oracle-wide abstention at gas cost).
- (ii) _Reveal starvation_: the FIFO (`ORDER BY id`) skips expired unsubmitted rows, so the backlog ahead of a `Reveal` is at most the still-unexpired `Approve`/`Commit` rows, ≈ 2 × proposals of the last `COMMIT_WINDOW` blocks; with 16 in flight per block and `REVEAL_WINDOW` blocks to drain, starvation needs roughly `2·R·CW > 16·RW`, i.e. more than ~8 sustained proposals per block when the windows are equal. Exposure is additionally capped by the victim's own fee-token balance (once `balance < bondTarget`, `commit` reverts and nothing is at risk), and `max_in_flight_transactions`/`blocks_before_resubmit` are operator-tunable (`tx/mod.rs:71-83`). Slashed funds go to the protocol receiver, not the attacker: griefing at attacker cost `fee` per request against victim loss `fee × slashingMultiplier` per starved sentinel.

**Verdict: Plausible.** Mechanism (no bound anywhere between proposal, engine, bond and queue) verified; the trigger is modelled, not shown — no queue-level or engine-level run. Certainty **55%**. **Severity Medium / Medium**: attacker-controlled but bounded griefing (linear in attacker spend, capped by the victim's balance), not an unbounded drain; not High. A16/A17 not engaged.

**QA suggestion.** A `TransactionQueue` test with `2R` `Commit` rows enqueued per block for `CW` blocks followed by `Reveal` rows expiring `RW` blocks later would turn (ii) into E1 without touching the engine.
